# Anthropic's IPO filing shows what frontier AI actually costs

AI-assisted starting point, author to edit.

Anthropic published its S-1 on Monday, October 26, 2026. IPO Scoop is the source for that date: the company “pulled back the curtain on its IPO by filing its long-awaited S-1 this morning.” The filing is a public look at Claude’s parent, and the figures that hold up across the grounded brief are about cash, compute, and control.

Bernama’s account of the filing says annual revenue grew from US$41 million in 2023 to US$386 million in 2024 and US$4.59 billion in 2025. Revenue in the second quarter of 2026 alone reached US$11.56 billion, and the company reported its first quarterly operating profit, US$974 million. Finimize, reading the same document, says third-quarter revenue was almost $17 billion, up 45% from the second quarter. It also reports a 10% adjusted operating margin in the first half of 2026, against a -4% GAAP operating margin, and an 11% third-quarter adjusted margin excluding stock-based compensation.

The loss lines are large, and a large share of them is accounting rather than cash burn. IPO Scoop states a net loss of $109.74 billion on revenue of $19.7 billion for the 12 months that ended on June 30, 2026. Bernama says the first-half 2026 net loss was US$80.7 billion, of which US$72.4 billion came from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and other financial instruments as the company’s valuation rose.

Cash on hand was already large before any offering. Finimize’s reading is “$112 billion of cash at the end of June, and a further $19 billion worth of short-term investments, making $132 billion before the IPO.”

The operating story is the infrastructure tab. Bernama reports that Anthropic spent nearly US$9 billion on computing resources and infrastructure in the first half of 2026 and has committed to US$518 billion in future infrastructure spending. The same report says Anthropic has agreed to spend at least US$110 billion with Amazon through April 2036 and at least US$114.5 billion with Google through July 2033.

Demand is not a single logo. IPO Scoop says that, as of September 30, 2026, more than 11,900 customers had $100,000 or more of revenue on a trailing-twelve-month basis. The S-1 did not disclose the terms of the IPO.

Founders kept the vote. Bernama reports that a single Class F share held by the seven co-founders carries 50.1 per cent of all stockholder voting rights.
